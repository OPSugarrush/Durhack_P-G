# App audit and improvement priorities

Date: 15 November 2026. Audited application commit: `e0d4235`, branch `audit/hackathon-brief-gap-review`. This report proposes changes; it does not implement them.

## Overall assessment

The app is a useful descriptive research prototype with an emerging business decision layer. Its strengths are traceable receipt evidence, conservative interpretation of brand histories, product resolution, and a much clearer separation between category opportunity, brand comparison and individual shopper views. It is not yet a validated forecasting or investment-return system. Its recommended actions are research suggestions rather than demonstrated prescriptions.

The biggest near-term improvement is reliability on the supplied workbook, followed by a consistent definition of evidence strength across screens. More charts or pages would have less value than those two improvements.

## Priority findings

### 1. P1 — Supplied-workbook import is not a reliable demo path yet

Evidence: the full test result above; `webapp.py:Store.run`, `workspace.py:analyse_lines`, `need_state/observations.py:replicated_pairs`. Frontend `waiting()` polls until completion with one generic progress message.

Impact: a judge can see an apparently stalled upload before reaching the best features. Passing the built-in 64-receipt sample is not sufficient for the actual challenge dataset.

Recommendation: instrument parse, resolution, pair analysis, per-basket inference, histories and report generation separately. Optimise the measured bottleneck, then expose stage progress and elapsed time. Avoid simply increasing the test timeout and calling it fixed. Acceptance: repeatable successful import of the full supplied workbook, with stage timings and a documented target duration on the demo machine. Keep small deterministic HTTP fixtures in routine CI and a separately identified full-workbook performance check.

### 2. P1 — Evidence thresholds differ between Findings and Business outlook

Evidence: `web/app.js:mainFinding` accepts category changes with two finding buyers and category q/interval checks; `workspace.py:analyse_lines` computes evidence once five comparable buyers exist; `business_outlook.py:business_outlook` additionally requires 20 comparable buyers and 10 category buyers before prioritising a test.

Impact: the same category can look like a supported finding on one screen and insufficient evidence on another. Different purposes can justify different thresholds, but that distinction is currently implicit.

Recommendation: one backend evidence classification used by all views, separating descriptive observation, statistically supported change and action readiness. Keep subgroup counts separate from population evidence. Also check directional agreement: a two-sided sign test concerns the number moving each way, while the bootstrap interval concerns mean magnitude. They can disagree when many small decreases coexist with fewer large increases. Store both quantities and explain disagreement rather than combining them into an uncomplicated verdict.

### 3. P2 — Missing category months are omitted rather than shown as zero observed purchases

Evidence: `business_outlook.py` builds `months` by iterating only months in category rows `g`.

Impact: if shoppers continue buying other things but make no recorded category purchases for a month, that month vanishes. This conceals an important decline.

Recommendation: construct the calendar from the eligible cohort's receipts, then reindex each category onto it. Show zero when receipts exist but the category does not; show no observations when there are no receipts. Test those cases separately. Monthly pooled receipt shares and the headline equal-buyer comparison also use different weighting, so their descriptions must remain distinct.

### 4. P2 — Brand comparisons need coverage and uncertainty for the actual comparison cohort

Evidence: `business_outlook.py` uses buyers with two category trips per period; `web/business.js:brandRows` uses five such buyers and two percentage points to highlight movement; `brandDetail` gates on 70% mapped item rows across the category.

Impact: these are display thresholds, not proof of a brand shift. Category-wide coverage can hide unequal brand recognition between periods or within the selected buyers. Five buyers can generate visually large unstable movements.

Recommendation: report per-period coverage and buyer support for each brand, add paired uncertainty, and suppress emphasis when resolution coverage changes substantially. Continue showing all-brand details as descriptive evidence. Never label the current rates as additive market share: multiple brands can appear on a trip.

### 5. P2 — Brand trials and switches are deliberately narrow, but wording needs finer distinctions

Evidence: `business_outlook.py` requires two category trips per period, complete brand mapping, and a sole repeated brand on each side for a possible switch. Other cases use `added` brands to choose a trial label. Owner comparisons correctly deduplicate receipts and distinguish a same-owner switch.

Impact: an alternating shopper who also tries one new brand receives only the trial label. A long gap and a rapid sequence can receive the same switch label. True new category buyers are often excluded from trial analysis by the two-category-trips-per-period rule.

Recommendation: preserve the cautious switch rule but represent trial, mixed buying and consistency as separate attributes. Show last old-brand and first new-brand dates, follow-up duration and observed repeat counts. Distinguish category entry from brand trial among existing category buyers. Do not equate silence with abandonment.

### 6. P2 — Business opportunity language can outrun the evidence

Evidence: `web/business.js:outlook` always describes the top-reach category as worth investigating; `business_outlook.py:actions` selects advice mainly from a direction/status label. The default sorted order includes unsupported descriptive movement after priority changes.

Impact: reach is not profitability, growth, unmet demand or P&G portfolio fit. The largest category may be a poor use of incremental company spending. Suggested actions are still similar across unrelated categories.

Recommendation: keep the useful reach/change separation, but attach a decision card only when it can name a specific product group, audience, plausible commercial lever and success metric. Offer a configurable portfolio lens using the existing `PortfolioLens`, with general market as a clear alternative. Do not invent costs, margin or uplift. A simple investment scenario should require user-supplied inputs and identify them as assumptions.

### 7. P2 — Spending needs stronger financial completeness handling

Evidence: `consumer_goals.py:spending_profiles` sums finite nonnegative line totals and shows missing-price coverage. It excludes refunds; a completely unpriced month sums to zero. `web/goals.js` blocks goal calculations when the latest month has missing prices, but headline totals remain numeric.

Impact: zero observed priced spending may look like zero spending. Partial months and absent receipts make the latest month a weak baseline. Returns can materially alter net spending.

Recommendation: show unavailable totals when no usable prices exist, label partially priced amounts as known subtotals, and separate gross purchases from refunds/net spend when supported. Prefer the most recent sufficiently observed completed period or allow the user to choose the reference. Keep consumer goals secondary to business analysis.

### 8. P2 — Continuous insight is still a manual cumulative-workbook process

Evidence: `webapp.py:Store.run` replaces one in-memory report; README documents loss on restart. `workspace.py:combine` exists but is unused in current imports.

Impact: no durable baseline, saved goals or follow-up measurements exist. The user must bring cumulative history to reproduce a comparison. This limits the brief's continuous engagement and long-term change outcomes.

Recommendation: preserve the simple one-file UI, but add an explicit local snapshot/save-and-reopen workflow and stable dataset identity. Store synthetic provenance, config version and analysis period with it. Reuse existing conflict-safe combination only if multi-file accumulation becomes a deliberate product choice.

### 9. P2 — Dataset changes and concurrent navigation can leave stale frontend state

Evidence: `web/app.js:waiting` clears `report` and assigns `location.hash='outlook'`, relying on a hash-change event to rerender. `route` caches the report. Server `Store` is shared by all tabs.

Impact: if the user moves to outlook while an import runs, completion can assign the same hash and fail to refresh the view. Another tab's import can change server finding indices while an older tab retains an old report.

Recommendation: explicit refresh after successful import, a dataset/version token on reports and evidence calls, and a clear refresh notice when a tab is stale. This is a code-review finding; it was not part of the earlier browser happy-path test.

### 10. P2 — Important reliability tools are stranded in the research interface

Evidence: `service.py:analyse` supplies hierarchy scans, portfolio annotations, discovery candidates and notes; missing-data/injection modules support stress tests. The custom workspace uses its own simpler category and pair path.

Impact: the project has more scientific support than a judge can discover through the primary app, while README can imply features are available in that app when they are primarily CLI/Streamlit capabilities.

Recommendation: a compact Evidence & limitations view linked from conclusions, summarising the relevant run's coverage, known limitations and validation results. Reuse calculations rather than porting every legacy chart or adding a parallel dashboard. Do not claim legacy validation directly validates newer brand/switch rules without running the appropriate checks.

### 11. P2 — The primary app lacks a reviewable business output

Evidence: `service.py:export` offers legacy analytical CSVs; custom `webapp.py:Handler` exposes state/findings/spending/evidence, with no business brief export.

Impact: a useful opportunity cannot easily be shared with teammates or judged as a specific decision recommendation.

Recommendation: export a concise brief for the selected category: conclusion, cohort/date range, evidence, alternatives, proposed experiment, success metric and limitations. This adds direct business value without another major screen.

### 12. P3 — UX hierarchy is better, but density and accessibility need another pass

Evidence: `web/business.js` places category and brand views in separate tabs, limits highlighted brands and paginates histories. Yet all categories render in the initial overview; the dedicated brand detail has a generic “Brand context” title and view-all content shows brands then owners together. Diverging bars scale to the largest current change without visible ticks.

Recommendation: show the top five categories with View all, make the selected category title explicit, use a Brands/Companies control when users need it, and label the change-axis scale. Put an explanatory small text summary beside charts and provide a table alternative. Check keyboard focus after category selection, contrast, zoom, and long product/company names. The mobile overflow test is useful but not a full accessibility audit.

### 13. P3 — Code organisation is modular, but compressed UI code increases change risk

Evidence: separate `business_outlook.py`, `consumer_goals.py`, `web/business.js` and `web/goals.js` avoid another data pipeline; many render functions and styles remain long single lines. Old `.opportunity` styles remain after the chart redesign.

Recommendation: format these files, extract small shared render helpers and remove unused styles in a standalone cleanup commit. Keep the three current UI modules; a framework migration is not justified. Remove/clearly mark unused combination paths and distinguish legacy versus current entry points in documentation.

### 14. P3 — Local-only safety is sensible; deployment and source flexibility are limited

Evidence: loopback binding, Host checks, request token, CSP, escaped dynamic strings, file-size and expanded-XLSX limits, synthetic declaration. `Store` is global in memory; downloads/uploads have no durable provenance record or per-user access model. Import accepts synthetic sources only.

Recommendation: keep the local deployment scope explicit. The brief also permits public nonpersonal datasets, so eventually allow that provenance category with a source/licence declaration. A checkbox does not detect real personal data. Public hosting would require a separate authentication, session isolation and resource-control design; it should not be an incidental hackathon change.

## Outcome-level judgement

**Descriptive:** strongest and substantially implemented. The app goes beyond raw totals through resolution, adjusted evidence and traceability, but these remain descriptions of past observations.

**Predictive:** not demonstrated. The goal percentage calculator is a scenario; mission inference describes an existing basket. Neither is a temporally validated estimate of future purchases. A modest forecast would need chronological holdouts, a simple baseline, error/coverage reporting and abstention for inadequate histories.

**Prescriptive:** emerging presentation, not demonstrated effectiveness. “Prioritise a test” is a sensible recommendation to learn, but current category advice lacks calibrated expected benefit or a completed decision-to-outcome loop. The deck's 1:1, 10:1 and 100:1 ROI levels are illustrations; none is measured here.

A convincing excellent-level demo could remain small: identify one supported opportunity, define a specific intervention and target cohort, show assumptions and a comparison group, then demonstrate how a later synthetic period evaluates the target outcome. Clearly distinguish simulated recovery from real business impact.

## Recommended delivery order

1. **Before relying on the full-workbook demo:** profile/fix import time; unify evidence labels; include zero-purchase months; refresh/version frontend data reliably.
2. **Improve the actual decision:** one category-specific experiment brief with export, portfolio relevance and clear alternatives. Keep the current page structure.
3. **Improve brand trust:** comparison-cohort coverage, uncertainty, dates/follow-up and separate trial/mixed attributes.
4. **Complete the consumer story:** known subtotal versus zero, useful reference period, saved synthetic goal/progress, one feasible suggested change rather than an arbitrary percentage alone.
5. **Then consider prediction:** a validated simple baseline, not a complex model added for presentation value.

Do not spend the next iteration on a framework rewrite, more generic charts, unsupported promotion causality, automated SKU discontinuation or a numeric investment score without defensible inputs.

## Scope limits of this audit

Reviewed current web app, ingestion, resolution/hierarchy interfaces, basket/mission logic, trend/brand/consumer modules, research services, reliability/validation infrastructure, tests, packaging, CI and documentation. This is source review plus the tests described above, not an exhaustive security assessment, causal validation, full accessibility certification or verified production deployment. Runtime performance findings apply to this machine/run. Proposed fixes are not silently implemented in this audit.
